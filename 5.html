Flavonoids

Structure, biosynthesis and pharmacology of plant polyphenols — condensed for revision, with diagrams, flash cards and a self-test quiz.

The essentials

Not the full lecture — just the rules, numbers and relationships worth carrying into the exam.

Definition

What a flavonoid is

An aromatic secondary plant metabolite, chemically a polyphenolic compound, built on C6-C3-C6 — 15 carbons total.

Core skeleton

Rings A, B, C

Two benzene rings (A and B) joined by a 3-carbon bridge, which commonly cyclizes with oxygen to form ring C (a pyran/pyrone).

Glycosides

Aglycone + glycone

Aglycone = pharmacological activity. Glycone (sugar) = no activity itself, but raises solubility and absorption.

Precursor

Chalcone

The true biosynthetic precursor of all flavonoids. Its 3-carbon chain is not cyclized, so chalcones themselves are not "true" flavonoids.

Substitution rule

Where -OH sits

Ring A: almost always 5-OH and 7-OH. Ring B: commonly 4′-OH, or both 3′ & 4′-OH.

Classification key

Flavone vs isoflavone

Ring B on C-2 of ring C → flavan/flavone family. Ring B on C-3 → iso-flavan/isoflavone family.

Major classes of plant phenols

C6Simple phenol — e.g. catechol
C6-C1Phenolic acids — e.g. salicylic acid
C6-C3Cinnamic acid derivatives
C6-C3-C6Flavonoids

Sources & role in the plant

Top dietary sourcesFruits, tea, and soybean
Green / black tea~25% flavonoids
AppleRich in quercetin
Citrus fruitsRich in rutin and hesperidin
Plant functionsPigmentation, UV screening, growth support, taste (bitter/sweet), defense

Biosynthesis in one line

Phenylalanine→ Cinnamic acid→ p-Coumaroyl-CoA+ 3 Malonyl-CoA → Chalcone→ Flavanone→ Flavone / Isoflavone / Flavonol / Anthocyanin

Mixed biosynthesis: ring B + the C3 bridge come from the shikimic acid pathway; ring A comes from the acetate–mevalonate pathway (3 × malonyl-CoA).

Direct radical scavenging — the equation

Flavonoid(OH) + R•  →  Flavonoid(O•) + RH R• = a free radical · O• = an oxygen free radical

Structure–activity rules for radical scavenging

Catechol on ring BGreat scavenging ability (ortho-dihydroxy)
Pyrogallol on ring BEven higher activity, e.g. myricetin (trihydroxy)
C2–C3 double bondStabilizes the radical formed
4-oxo groupBoosts activity, especially with the C2–C3 bond
3-OH groupExtremely active in combination with the above
5-OH & 7-OHAdd scavenging potential in some cases

Pharmacological actions at a glance

AntioxidantDirect radical scavenging + indirect iNOS inhibition, blocking peroxynitrite formation; iron chelation (quercetin)
Anti-inflammatoryInhibit COX & 5-LOX, reduce eicosanoid/prostaglandin synthesis, inhibit neutrophil degranulation
AntitumorInhibit cell proliferation; reduce heat-shock proteins; inhibit tyrosine kinases (quercetin); quercetin & kaempferol block DNA/RNA/protein synthesis in tumor cells
HepatoprotectiveSilymarin, quercetin & rutin raise hepatic glutathione, aiding detoxification and hepatocyte repair
VascularRutin, hesperidin & quercetin = "Vitamin P" — reduce capillary fragility

Drugs & examples to remember

SilymarinFlavanonolignan mixture (silybin, silychristin, silydianin) from milk thistle seeds — hepatoprotective
QuercetinAntioxidant; inhibits iNOS, COX-2 and xanthine oxidase (gout)
MyricetinStrong protection against carcinogenic mutation
HesperidinCitrus glycoside (aglycone: hesperetin) — "Vitamin P"; in Daflon 500 mg
GenisteinIsoflavonoid; acts as a phytoestrogen
